Confirmed local defect and fix
AAC-LC access units from the tested cameras represent 1024 samples at 16 kHz
(64 ms). Source timestamps jitter around that cadence, sometimes almost zero
apart. FFmpeg's MP4 writer compares a new fragment DTS against the previous
sample's END, not only against its starting DTS; an overlap causes duration
warnings, one-tick samples and missing PTS.
Reference implementation: FFmpeg n8.0 libavformat/movenc.c, check_pkt:
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/n8.0/libavformat/movenc.c

For explicitly enabled Reolink timestamp normalization only, ordinary two-byte
AAC-LC ASC with a matching sample-rate timebase now uses a sample-exact clock.
Supports both 1024- and 960-sample frame flags. Encoded payload is untouched.
Unknown/extended ASC, other audio codecs, other camera profiles and recording
output retain their existing paths. Forward gaps beyond 16 access units are
preserved; distant backward resets are rejected rather than rewritten forever.
This is deliberately bounded, not a blanket ignore-errors policy.

Measurements
Camera 13 original 120-second mux capture: 1,833 AAC packets, including 126
one-tick durations; 1,236 durations differed from 1024 samples.
Camera 13 repaired 15-minute capture: 14,017 AAC packets, zero one-tick samples,
only two non-1024 durations (initial mux alignment and a retained discontinuity).
Camera 11 repaired 15-minute capture: 14,020 AAC packets, zero one-tick samples,
only the initial alignment duration differed from 1024. Both had strictly
increasing demuxed audio DTS, with no separate gaps after sample durations.

Both 15-minute live preview tests: no reconnect/generation change, no established
video packet drops, no mux errors, zero negative audio-duration warnings,
zero missing-PTS warnings and zero FFmpeg errors. Each had one startup video
drop/recovery. Camera 13 had two output timestamp safety rejections (audio),
and ended around 1.06 seconds A/V skew; camera 11 had none and about 137 ms.
Do not represent these as proof of zero audio loss or zero long-session drift.

Thirty-second delayed input/output audits on each camera: 1,069 input packets
and decisions, all written, no pre-mux payload changes, hash mismatches or
writer queue rejections. One unmatched source correlation per capture is the
pending packet straddling the capture boundary.

Browser validation and an important discarded hypothesis
The first replay fixture accidentally omitted audio-only moofs, which made
Firefox stall repeatedly at the same point. Correcting that fixture removed
the artificial stall. Trials of segments mode and disabling back-buffer removal
were reverted; the shipped browser player is unchanged.
The original 90-second capture and repaired three-minute camera-13 capture
both played in Firefox 156 without stalls/restarts/errors. The repaired
camera-11 capture also passed three minutes in Chromium (two startup rendering
drops, no stalls/restarts/errors). Longer and final-build results follow below.
Replay preserves every moof/mdat, but schedules by video time; it is not a
reproduction of original network-arrival jitter or exact partial boundaries.
