Confirmed recovery-policy mismatch
Camera 11 had 8 client restarts and camera 13 had 69; the latest reasons were
drift, with no reported client stalls/errors. Both selected H.264/AAC substreams.
The server explicitly disables keyframeSeekSafe when audio is muxed. Their
four-second GOP is therefore NOT the immediate blocker: there are no eligible
seek targets at all. Do not simply enable the flag; buffered boundaries are
not reliable exact video random-access timestamps for multi-track media.
The client accelerates to 1.08x but restarts after >5 seconds of buffered
headroom for >3 seconds. It can restart while playback progresses and lag falls.
Normal 90-second saved camera-13 replays passed Firefox and Chromium. A
controlled six-second delivery pause produced a stuck restart, not drift.
A controlled backwards seek of 4.2 seconds produced catchUpStart at about
5.60 seconds and driftRestart around 5.29 seconds, without stalls or errors.
This reproduces the recovery decision, NOT the production origin of the lag.

Inspection findings / hypotheses
1. useMseStream uses SourceBuffer sequence mode for muxed audio/video. The
   embedded media timing and coded-frame group handling determine actual
   buffered advancement; partial.duration is metadata, not a client clock.
   Saved replay preserves all moof/mdat data but changes append boundaries and
   schedules by video time. It cannot rule out production boundary/jitter
   effects. Compare buffer advancement, timestamps and audio/video fragments
   against wall-clock playback before considering a segments-mode change.
2. Initial reserve uses the first buffered start and last buffered end. With
   disjoint ranges, this can count a gap as playable reserve. The drift watchdog
   similarly measures to the last range rather than the range containing the
   playhead. New range snapshots will show whether these assumptions matter.
3. Client appends are serialized and byte/count bounded. A zero queue in a
   recovered snapshot does not rule out a preceding burst; periodic interval
   counters and retained pre-restart history now cover that missing evidence.
4. StreamBroadcaster's slow-client guard does not bound asynchronous sends.
   Its single broadcast thread increments pending immediately before send,
   and decrements immediately after send returns. Installed Crow websocket.h
   send_data posts to the connection io_context, which then queues write_buffers
   and performs async writes. Thus the 128-pending guard cannot observe actual
   outstanding network messages/bytes. The broadcaster's own outbound queue
   also has no size/age limit. This is a genuine inspection finding, but NOT
   proof it caused the selective Reolink drift. Actual transport completion/
   queue age requires separate work; do not drop arbitrary encoded partials.
5. Browser suspension resets/adjusts watchdog timers, but does not magically
   eliminate playback lag. Playback/mute/visibility and scheduling evidence
   is needed to distinguish this from media timeline growth or delivery bursts.
